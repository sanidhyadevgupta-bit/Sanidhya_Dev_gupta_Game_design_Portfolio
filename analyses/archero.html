carnival of arrows

Archero · Habby · live-ops event design

A weekly event designed to get one in four lapsed one-time payers to buy a second time, without raising the energy cap or selling them a bigger version of their first purchase.

▶ Play the prototype ↗ Alternate theme: the Reliquary ↗

01What it is

Archero is a top-down roguelite: your hero fires on their own, you only move, and every level-up you draft one of three abilities. Around that sits a deep free-to-play meta (talents, gear, heroes, pets) and a calendar of timed events. This was a Design Management 2 exercise at NID: first take an existing event apart, then design a new one to a real business brief.

02The teardown: Ancient Maze

Before designing anything, my group (with Sagar and Navneet) took apart Archero's Ancient Maze event, which arrived in update 2.5. It's a 100-room gauntlet that strips your gear stats and gates entry behind energy.

Pinch: where you feel forced to spend

A 15-energy entry is 75% of the base cap of 20. The 100-gem refresh appears at the near-miss moment, after a long run fails late.

Pull: what makes you want to

Hero shards are scarce everywhere else, a 6-day shop refresh creates a deadline, and the rare orange portal works like a slot machine.

Gaps we found: no social or co-op layer, no record of past runs, and no way in for casual players who can't commit to a full 100-room session.

03The brief

Goal1 in 4 one-time payers buys again within 3 weeks: about 1,000 of 4,005 players
SegmentOne-time payers who spent under $5 and stopped: 62% of all payers, ARPPU $11.60
GuardrailsPayer conversion stays at or above 1.9%; rewarded ads per DAU must not fall further
ConstraintNo global energy-cap raise; the second purchase can't be a bigger version of the first

04The design

A recurring 7-day event that runs three times across the KPI window, built from two connected systems:

  • 7 daily challenges
  • →
  • All 7 done = 1 arrow
  • →
  • Shoot the balloon board
  • →
  • Reward tier revealed
  • →
  • Tracker fills, pity at 7
  • The daily checklist. Seven challenges, fresh every day. Each one can be played, rerolled for gold (150, 200, 250…) or skipped for 80 gems. Only a full 7/7 earns that day's arrow. Missing a day costs nothing but that arrow.
  • The carnival board. Nine balloons in a fixed dartboard: a small bullseye weighted toward epic and grand rewards, and bigger, easier rings weighted toward common ones. Aiming is a real risk-and-reward choice, and a pity system guarantees the grand prize within 7 shots.
  • Reward ladder. Common (gear fragments), Rare (upgrade stone), Epic (a skin), Grand (a permanent stat upgrade and a title).
  • The monetisation hook. Gems skip the last challenge of the day, or buy arrows outright (1 for 50, 5 for 235). Both sit exactly at the daily gate. That meets the brief: it's a new reason to buy, not a bigger first purchase.

05What I'd steal and what I'd change

Steal

A purchase placed at a moment the player chose to reach (6 of 7 done), not at a moment of loss. And a pity timer stated up front, so the odds feel fair.

Still open

The gem and gold prices are placeholders to check against Archero's live economy. I also need to decide whether ring-weighted odds should stay, or whether odds should be the same everywhere on the board.

06Where it shows up in my games

Archero's loop (auto-fire, move to survive, draft one of three) is the same family as TERMINUS. The teardown sharpened one rule I kept there: progress should never depend on paying or on punishing a bad run. In TERMINUS a death still keeps 75% of your scrap.
